German Requirements for University Admission

Plain-English summaries and official links for seven universities in Switzerland, Germany and Austria.

Sources checked 27 September 2026. Requirements change — always confirm on the university's own page before applying.

Switzerland

ETH Zurich

Applicants with a foreign (non-Swiss) school-leaving certificate must show proof of German alongside their certificate recognition, from a defined list of recognised certificates with age limits and possible waivers. ETH itself does not run preparatory language courses or exams for applicants — that preparation has to happen elsewhere before applying. Medicine has its own, separate application procedure.

Germany

Technical University of Munich (TUM)

TUM lists standard and exceptional accepted German certificates, and recognises IB German A at Higher Level, plus a specified "GIB" route, among qualifying school evidence. Even some English-taught bachelor programmes still ask for a baseline — the Aerospace BSc, for example, requires German A2 by the application deadline. Students who need to build up their German through TUM's Studienkolleg preparatory year need B2 before applying, plus an entrance exam. TUM's own Language Center does not run admissions-prep courses, TestDaF or DSH exams for applicants.

Germany

Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich (LMU)

LMU accepts a defined list of German certificates, most without an expiry date, and exempts students whose prior schooling already demonstrates German; exactly which proof is needed depends on the application route. LMU's official information sheet for non-EU applicants sets a final-proof deadline for programmes with restricted admission and a B2 pathway for other applications. Students who still need to reach the required level can use LMU's officially linked external language courses and DSH/TestDaF preparation.

Austria

University of Vienna

Depending on the programme, the University of Vienna asks for German at A2 or at C1, unless an applicant is exempt by declaring German as a first language. The C1 evidence list has a three-year validity limit and accepts some educational proof; students who enter via the VWU preparatory programme must also pass the EPD German exam afterwards. An IB diploma with German automatically counts as A2 evidence, but not automatically as C1 evidence. The separate A2 list has a shorter two-year validity window. Students who aren't there yet can join the University Preparation Programme and later sit a binding supplementary German exam, and some programmes with their own entrance procedures set additional language conditions on top of the general rule.

Switzerland

University of Geneva

Geneva teaches primarily in French, and its general undergraduate language exam and exemptions cover French, not German — German requirements sit inside specific programmes instead. The Bachelor in German Language, Literature and Civilisation expects roughly B2 on entry under its approved study plan, with a placement test and a dedicated first-year language module; students below B2 can use the German department's supported practical classes. Separately, the Faculty of Translation and Interpreting sets German-as-a-working-language conditions as part of its language-combination and admission-test rules for its current application cycle, and a 2023 document on B-language test waivers is still linked from that admissions page.

Germany

Heidelberg University

Heidelberg's general page for international applicants lists standard accepted certificates and the GIB route, and notes that some programmes require proof earlier or set stricter thresholds than the general rule. Several subjects publish their own, higher bar: Medicine, Dentistry, Pharmacy and Psychology have a dedicated sheet with exact higher score thresholds for specified non-EU/EEA applicants; Comparative German Studies (Germanistik im Kulturvergleich) sets enhanced scores with a conditional pathway; Law's applicant-selection regulation requires DSH-3 or an equivalent certificate; and Physiotherapy Science's regulation sets its own higher requirement with defined exemptions. Students not yet at the required level can join preparatory German courses at Heidelberg's International Study Centre, which lead toward the DSH exam or the Studienkolleg, and the DSH exam itself has separate written and oral thresholds that determine access to study.

Austria

Medical University of Vienna

Medicine and Dentistry at MedUni Vienna require German at C1, with formal admission only finalised after a successful entrance-exam result. The accepted C1 evidence list includes exemptions for certain educational backgrounds and its own certificate age limits, formalised in the Rectorate's regulation, which also sets A2-level proof for the non-degree preparatory route (Vorstudienlehrgang) with its own age limits. Students entering through that preparatory route need only A2 at entry but must then pass the compulsory EPD German exam during their studies; the IB diploma is listed as its own type of educational document among the accepted evidence. Separately, the joint MedAT entrance exam used across Austrian medical universities includes German-language test material of its own, distinct from the university's formal C1 admission requirement — and a MedAT result is only valid for the following academic year.

About TestDaF and combined certificates

TestDaF is one of the certificates several universities above accept as evidence. It reports separate skill scores and now offers a digital combined certificate option — but it's each university that decides whether it recognises that option, so check the relevant page above rather than assuming.
